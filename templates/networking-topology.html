Network Topologies

A topology is the layout of devices and the links between them.

{% endblock %} {% block content %}

Star

Every device connects to a central switch. Easy to manage and a failed cable affects one device only. If the switch fails, the whole network stops. The most common LAN layout.

Bus

All devices share one backbone cable. Cheap and simple, but one break splits the network and performance drops as devices are added. Mostly historical.

Ring

Each device connects to exactly two neighbours, forming a loop. Data travels in one direction (or both in a dual ring). Used in some fibre backbones.

Mesh

Devices have direct links to many others. Very reliable because traffic can take another path, but expensive to cable. Used between routers on the internet.

Tree (hierarchical)

Stars connected in layers: core, distribution and access. Scales well for buildings and campuses. A failure high in the tree isolates everything below it.

Hybrid

Real networks mix layouts: for example stars at each office joined by a partial mesh of routers. Our simulator sample labs use this approach.
